Lead generation for bathroom fitters is rarely a numbers problem. Most fitters get enough enquiries to keep going. The real problem is the gap between someone filling in a form at 9pm and you finally getting back to them the next afternoon, by which point they have already messaged two other fitters and booked a site visit with whoever replied first.

A full bathroom refit, a wet room conversion or an accessible bathroom is a considered purchase. The person enquiring has usually been thinking about it for weeks, has a rough budget in mind, and wants reassurance that someone competent will turn up, do the job properly and not leave them without a working bathroom for a month. Speed and clarity in that first conversation matter as much as the quote itself.

## The problem bathroom fitters actually have

Most bathroom fitters get work from a mix of referrals, a Facebook page, Checkatrade or similar directories, and whatever comes through the website. The trouble with this mix is that it is unpredictable and hard to control. A quiet January can follow a busy autumn with no warning, and there is no reliable way to turn the tap up when you need more work in the diary.

There is also a qualification problem. A general enquiry form or a Facebook message gives you a name and maybe a phone number, nothing else. You do not know if they want a full refit or just a new toilet seat, whether they have a budget that matches a proper wet room installation, or whether they are three months away from actually starting. Ringing round every enquiry to find this out eats into evening and weekend time that most fitters would rather spend on the tools or with family.```

And then there is the reply speed. Someone searching for a bathroom fitter at 8pm on a Sunday is often comparing two or three options at once. Whoever replies first, with something more useful than 'thanks, I'll call you back tomorrow', tends to win the enquiry. If your only way of picking up messages is checking your phone between jobs, you are losing some of these before you even see them.

## How lead generation for bathroom fitters works step by step

The system that solves this has four parts, and each one exists to fix a specific gap in the process above.

1. **Targeted ads.** Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) are shown to homeowners in your working area, often based on the kind of job you want more of: full refits, wet rooms, accessible bathrooms or tiling. The ad can show before and after photos of real work, which does more for a bathroom fitter than almost any other format of advert.
2. **An instant lead form.** Instead of sending people to a website contact page they may never finish filling in, the ad opens a short form inside Facebook or Instagram itself. A few quick questions: rough budget, postcode, type of job, when they want it done. This removes friction and gets more people to actually submit an enquiry.
3. **An AI WhatsApp agent.** As soon as the form is submitted, an AI agent messages the lead on WhatsApp within seconds, not hours. It asks the qualifying questions a human would ask on a first call: is this a full refit or a wet room, what is the rough budget, what postcode is the job in, and roughly when do they want to start. It does this in a natural, conversational way rather than firing a list of questions at once.
4. **A booking straight into your calendar.** Once a lead is qualified as a genuine fit, the agent offers a site visit or a call and books it directly into your calendar. You open your phone and see a confirmed appointment with someone who has already said what they want, what they can spend and when they need it doing, rather than a cold number you have to ring and start the conversation from scratch.

This whole flow can run alongside a website chatbot that does the same job for people who find you through Google or your own site, so visitors get the same instant response whichever route they come in through. For a fitter whose current website does not generate many enquiries at all, it is worth looking at this [guide to websites for bathroom fitters](/blog/websites-for-bathroom-fitters-in-rochdale) alongside the ad side, since a slow or dated site will quietly undo some of the good work the ads are doing.

## What good looks like

A well-run system for a bathroom fitter produces a small, steady stream of enquiries each week rather than a flood that overwhelms you or a trickle that leaves you guessing. The ads are specific enough that someone who only wants a cheap toilet swap self-selects out, because the ad and the form are clearly talking about full refits, wet rooms or accessible bathrooms.

By the time you see a booked appointment, you already know the postcode, the type of job and a sensible budget range. A job in a terraced house needing a full refit with new tiling looks different from a wet room conversion for an elderly relative with grab rails and level access, and the qualifying questions should make that distinction obvious before you ever leave the house to quote.

The AI agent should sound like a helpful person, not a script. It should be able to handle the ordinary follow-up questions: do you do wet rooms, do you supply materials or just fit, how soon could someone come and look. If a lead asks something it cannot answer sensibly, it should hand over to you rather than giving a vague or unhelpful reply. ## Common mistakes to avoid

A few mistakes come up repeatedly when bathroom fitters try to set this up themselves or with the wrong supplier.

- **Running the same ad to everyone.** A fitter who specialises in wet rooms and accessible bathrooms needs different messaging and photos from one chasing standard three-piece refits. One generic ad for all services usually performs worse than two or three specific ones.
- **Sending leads to a long form.** Every extra field on a form loses a percentage of people who were about to enquire. Keep it to what you genuinely need to qualify the lead: postcode, job type, rough budget, timing.
- **Treating the WhatsApp reply as a one-off.** A good system follows up over several days if someone goes quiet, the same way a persistent but polite salesperson would, rather than giving up after one unanswered message.
- **No budget filter.** Without a budget question, you end up quoting full refits to people who were only ever going to spend a few hundred pounds, which wastes site visit time that could go to genuine jobs.
- **Ignoring the follow through after the job is won.** The leads that turn into quotes still need chasing, so it is worth looking at how [automation for bathroom fitters](/blog/automation-for-bathroom-fitters-that-chases-quotes-for-you) can chase those quotes automatically rather than relying on memory.

## How to judge whether it is working

Judge the system on the quality and speed of appointments reaching your diary, not just the raw number of enquiries. A smaller number of well-qualified bookings for full refits and wet rooms is worth more than a large number of vague messages that go nowhere.

Track how many booked appointments turn into quotes, and how many quotes turn into won jobs. If a high proportion of leads are the wrong fit, that points to the ad targeting or the qualifying questions needing adjustment, not necessarily the whole approach. If leads are well matched but still not converting into booked appointments, the issue is more likely to be reply speed or how the conversation is handled once someone replies.

It is also worth keeping an eye on cost per booked job rather than cost per enquiry. A fitter paying more per lead but getting mostly full refits and wet rooms is usually better off than one paying less for a stream of small, low value jobs that take just as much time to quote.

## Frequently asked questions

### How quickly can lead generation for bathroom fitters start producing enquiries?

Once the ads are approved and the AI WhatsApp agent is set up, enquiries typically start coming through within days rather than weeks. How many you get depends on your working area, your budget and how competitive bathroom fitting is locally, but the system itself does not need a long warm up period to start working.

### Will the AI WhatsApp agent put off older customers who want a human conversation?

Most people, including older homeowners, are used to messaging on WhatsApp and generally prefer a fast, clear reply to waiting hours for a phone call. The agent is there to qualify the lead and get a site visit booked quickly, not to replace the proper human conversation you will have on that visit.

### Do I need a new website before I start running ads?

Not necessarily, since the ads can send people straight into a lead form rather than your website. That said, a slow or outdated website can still cost you enquiries from people who search for you directly, so it is worth reviewing your [website for bathroom fitters](/blog/websites-for-bathroom-fitters-in-rochdale) alongside setting up ads.

### How is this different from just boosting a Facebook post?

Boosting a post puts it in front of more people but does nothing to capture or qualify the response. This system combines targeted ads with an instant lead form and automatic follow-up, so every enquiry gets a fast reply and is sorted by budget, job type and timing before it reaches your diary.

### Can this handle both small jobs like tiling and full wet room conversions?

Yes, though it works best when you run separate campaigns for different job types, since the questions and photos that attract someone wanting a full wet room conversion differ from those that attract someone who just wants a bathroom retiled.
