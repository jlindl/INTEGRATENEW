Most plastering enquiries come in at the worst possible moment. You're halfway up a ladder with a hawk in one hand, or you've just mixed a bag of bonding and you've got twenty minutes before it goes off. An AI WhatsApp assistant for plasterers solves a specific problem: it replies to every new enquiry within moments, asks the questions you'd ask yourself, and only puts the good ones in front of you, already booked in.

This isn't about replacing conversations with customers. It's about making sure nobody slips through the gap between when they fill in a form and when you've got five minutes free to call them back. For a one or two-person plastering business, that gap is where a lot of winnable work quietly disappears to a competitor who happened to reply first.

## Why plasterers lose work before they even quote

Plastering enquiries are often time-sensitive in a way other trades don't always face. Someone has had a leak, pulled off a ceiling, or just had a kitchen ripped out and needs the walls skimmed before the fitters arrive on a fixed date. They're not browsing. They're trying to find someone who can actually turn up.

If three plasterers get the same enquiry and you're the one who replies two hours later because you were sheeting up a room, you're often out of the running before you've said a word. The homeowner has already had a conversation with someone else, maybe already agreed a date.

There's also a filtering problem. Not every enquiry is a good fit. Someone wants a single small patch repair on a Tuesday afternoon for £60. Someone else is asking about render for a whole house extension. Someone wants coving matched to an old Victorian ceiling and has unrealistic expectations about cost. Working out which of these is worth your time usually means a phone call, and phone calls take time you don't have spare between jobs.

## How an AI WhatsApp assistant for plasterers works step by step

The mechanics are straightforward, even though what's happening behind the scenes is fairly sophisticated.

1. A lead comes in, typically from a Facebook or Instagram ad, a website enquiry form, or a Google search landing on your site.
2. The AI agent messages them on WhatsApp within moments, introducing itself as part of your business and starting a conversation, not sending a generic auto-reply.
3. It asks the questions that actually matter for a plastering job: what kind of work (skimming, re-plastering, rendering, coving, repairs), the size or number of rooms, the postcode or area, roughly when they want it done, and whether they have a budget in mind.
4. It uses the answers to work out whether this is a job worth your time. A full render on a detached house in your usual working area is a strong fit. A single-room skim fifty miles away with a date that's already passed might not be.
5. Good-fit leads get booked straight into your calendar for a call-back or a site visit, at a time that suits you, with all the details already captured.
6. Poor-fit leads are handled politely and don't take up any of your time at all.

The conversation reads naturally. A homeowner replies, the assistant replies back within seconds, and by the end they've given you everything you'd normally have to extract over a phone call: what the job is, where it is, and roughly when they want it starting. You open your calendar in the evening and see three qualified call-backs waiting, not seventeen unread messages and a missed call from an unknown number.

This is the same underlying approach used for other trades. ## What good looks like for a plastering business

A well set-up assistant feels like a sensible, slightly formal member of staff, not a robot reading a script. For plasterers specifically, that means the questions are tailored to the actual jobs you do.

For a re-plastering enquiry, it asks how many rooms, whether the existing plaster is being hacked off or skimmed over, and whether there's any damp involved, because that changes the job and the price. For rendering, it asks about the size of the elevation, the type of property, and whether there's existing render to remove. For coving, it asks about room dimensions and whether they want a simple profile or something more decorative to match period features. For a straightforward skim, it confirms room size, ceiling height, and when they need access.

Good also means speed without sounding rushed. The first message should land within a minute or two of the enquiry coming in, at any hour, because people fill in forms in the evening and at weekends just as often as during the working day. And it means the handover to you is clean: a qualified lead arrives in your calendar with the job type, location and timing already attached, so when you call them back you're confirming details, not starting from scratch.

## Common mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake is treating the assistant as a one-way notification system rather than a genuine qualifying conversation. If it just confirms receipt of an enquiry without asking anything useful, you've automated the easy part and left the hard part, working out if it's worth your time, exactly where it was before.

Another common mistake is setting the qualifying questions too generically. A script built for a general trade will ask vague questions about 'the job' instead of asking about room count, render type or coving profile. The more specific the questions, the more useful the answers are when you come to quote.

Some businesses also set the filtering too tight, rejecting anything slightly outside the usual job size, and end up turning away work that would have been perfectly fine to take on in a quiet week. It's worth reviewing what counts as a good fit every few months, especially if your diary looks different in winter compared with summer.

And a few businesses set it up once and never check the conversations it's actually having. Reading through a sample of exchanges every so often tells you whether it's asking the right things and sounding like your business, not a template that was never adjusted.

## How to judge whether it's working

The most useful measure isn't how many messages the assistant sends. It's whether the leads landing in your calendar are ones you'd have wanted anyway, and whether you're spending less time chasing people who were never going to book.

A few practical checks worth doing after a month or two:

- Are qualified leads arriving with the right information attached, so you're not having to ask the same basic questions again on the phone?
- Has the number of enquiries you lose to 'went with someone else, they replied quicker' gone down?
- Are you still getting a sensible mix of job types, skims, re-plasters, render, coving, rather than the filtering accidentally cutting out a type of work you still want?
- Is the conversation tone matching how you'd actually talk to a customer, rather than sounding stiff or overly formal?
- Are you checking your calendar once a day and finding it already has useful information in it, rather than a list of names and numbers to decode?

If the answer to most of these is yes, it's doing its job. If not, the fix is usually adjusting the questions or the qualifying criteria rather than scrapping the approach altogether.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the AI sound like a real person or obviously automated?

A well-built assistant is written in plain, natural language and responds to what the customer actually says, rather than following a rigid script. Most homeowners don't mind that it's automated as long as it's quick, polite and useful. What matters more than sounding human is replying fast and asking sensible questions.

### Will it put off customers who prefer to just phone?

No, it works alongside your phone number rather than replacing it. Some customers will still call, and that's fine. The assistant catches the enquiries that come in through forms and ads, which otherwise tend to sit unanswered for hours.

### Can it handle quotes for jobs like rendering, which vary a lot in price?

It isn't meant to give a firm quote. Its job is to gather enough detail, property type, elevation size, existing render condition, so that when you do call back or visit, you're already most of the way to a figure, rather than starting the conversation from zero.

### What happens to leads it decides are a poor fit?

They're usually handled politely without taking up your time, for example being told the job is outside your current working area or job size, or pointed towards booking in further ahead if your diary is full. You can review these periodically to make sure the filtering is set correctly.

### How quickly does it reply to a new enquiry?

Typically within a minute or two, at any time of day, which is the main advantage over checking messages between jobs. Speed of reply is one of the biggest factors in whether a plastering enquiry turns into a booked job.
