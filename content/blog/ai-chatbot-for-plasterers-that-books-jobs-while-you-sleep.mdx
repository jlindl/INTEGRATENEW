Most plastering enquiries don't come in during working hours. They come in at half seven in the evening, when someone's just noticed a crack running across the ceiling and starts searching for a plasterer nearby. If your website doesn't answer them there and then, they move to the next name on the list. An AI chatbot for plasterers is built to catch exactly this moment: it answers the visitor's questions, works out what job they actually need, and gets them booked in, all before you've even seen the notification.

This is not the same as a contact form that sits there until Monday morning. It's a live conversation on your website that behaves the way a good office manager would, if you had one on call around the clock.

## Why plastering enquiries fall through the gap

Plastering work is rarely urgent in the way a burst pipe is, but it's often booked in a hurry. Someone's had a wall knocked through, a builder's finished first fix and needs a skim before decorators go in, or a survey has flagged damp and a re-plaster is now on the to-do list. These decisions tend to get made in the evening, once the day job is done and the kids are in bed, which is exactly when most tradespeople are not looking at their phone.

A website with just a phone number and a contact form loses a chunk of these visitors. They fill in a form and hear nothing back for a day, or they can't tell from the site whether you do rendering as well as internal skimming, or whether you take on smaller jobs like patching and coving. Rather than wait, they ring the next plasterer in the search results.

The same problem shows up for [roofers](/blog/websites-for-roofers-in-bolton-that-win-more-local-work), [fencing contractors](/blog/websites-for-fencing-contractors-that-win-more-enquiries) and plumbers, but it hits plasterers particularly hard because the range of jobs is so wide. A visitor asking about coving needs a very different answer to one asking about a full re-plaster after fire or flood damage, and a generic contact form can't tell the difference.

## AI chatbot for plasterers: how it actually works, step by step

The mechanics are straightforward, even if the technology behind them isn't.

1. **A visitor lands on your site**, usually from a Google search like 'plasterer near me' or from a Facebook or Instagram advert.
2. **The chatbot opens a conversation**, either straight away or once the visitor has been on the page for a few seconds. It asks what kind of job they need: a skim, a re-plaster, rendering, coving, or something else.
3. **It asks the follow-up questions a plasterer would ask on the phone**: which room or how much wall area, whether the property is occupied or empty, and roughly when they'd like the work done. For rendering, it might ask about the type of wall and whether there's existing render to remove.
4. **It checks location and fit.** If someone's well outside your working area, it can say so politely rather than booking a job you'd have to turn down later.
5. **It qualifies budget in general terms**, giving a sense of scale rather than a fixed quote, since plastering prices depend on condition of the surface and access.
6. **It books a slot.** If the enquiry looks like a good fit, it offers a call-back time or a site visit directly into your calendar, so there's no back-and-forth over email trying to find a time that works.
7. **It hands you a clean summary** by WhatsApp or email: job type, rough size, location, timing, and anything unusual (an insurance claim, a listed building, a tight access).

This is the same qualifying logic used by an AI WhatsApp agent responding to Meta ad leads, just applied to people who find you through your own website rather than through an advert. Many plastering businesses run both at once: adverts driving traffic and enquiries in, and the website assistant catching everyone who visits directly, searches your name, or gets referred by a builder or previous customer.

## What good looks like for a plastering business

A well set up assistant should feel like a knowledgeable pair of hands on the phone, not a script.

- It should know the difference between a small patch repair, a full skim of a room, and a re-plaster after removing artex or wallpaper, and ask the right questions for each.
- It should recognise rendering enquiries as a separate job type, since these often involve scaffolding, weather windows and a different pricing structure to internal work.
- It should be honest about what it doesn't know. Rather than guessing at a price, it should confirm the details are enough to arrange a proper quote or site visit.
- It should hand off cleanly to a real person for anything unusual, like structural cracking, damp that needs investigating before plastering, or a job for a housing association or letting agent.
- It should book straight into your diary, not just promise someone will 'be in touch'.

The test is simple: could you read the summary it sends you and go straight to the job without having to ring the customer back just to ask the basics? If yes, it's doing its job.

## Common mistakes to avoid

A few things trip up plastering businesses when they first set this up.

- **Asking too many questions.** If the chatbot interrogates every visitor with ten questions before offering anything useful, people give up. Three or four well-chosen questions beat a long form.
- **Treating every job the same.** A skim coat on a small bathroom and a full re-render on a bungalow are different conversations. A chatbot that uses one generic script for both will miss detail that matters.
- **No route to a human.** Some enquiries, particularly damp-related re-plastering or insurance work, need a real conversation early on. The assistant should recognise these and flag them rather than trying to handle everything itself.
- **Not connecting it to your actual diary.** If the assistant books a slot that clashes with a job you already have on, you've just created admin instead of removing it.
- **Bolting it onto a slow or dated website.** If the site itself takes too long to load or doesn't work properly on a phone, most visitors won't stick around long enough to start the conversation. Speed and mobile design matter just as much as the assistant itself, which is part of why this usually gets built alongside a proper [web design](/web-design) project rather than added as an afterthought.

## How to judge whether it's working

Once it's live, there are a few practical ways to check it's earning its keep.

- **Look at when leads come in.** If you start seeing genuine enquiries arriving at 9pm or on a Sunday afternoon, that's work you'd previously have lost.
- **Check the quality of the summaries.** You should be able to see job type, location and rough scope for every enquiry, not just a name and a vague message.
- **Track how many enquiries turn into booked jobs**, not just how many conversations the chatbot has. A high number of chats with a low booking rate usually means the questions or the handoff need adjusting.
- **Read a sample of transcripts occasionally.** This shows you where visitors get confused, drop off, or ask something the assistant doesn't handle well yet, so it can be improved.
- **Compare it to your old contact form**, if you kept one running for a while. The difference in response speed and completeness of information is usually the clearest sign of whether it's paying for itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will an AI chatbot understand specific plastering terms like coving or artex removal?

Yes, when it's set up properly for the trade. It's built around the actual jobs plasterers quote for, including skimming, re-plastering, rendering, coving and repair work, so it asks relevant follow-up questions rather than generic ones.

### Does this replace my phone number?

No. It sits alongside it, catching the enquiries that would otherwise happen outside your working hours or get lost to a slow contact form. Anyone who prefers to ring can still do so.

### Can it give customers an actual price?

It can give a general sense of scale, but plastering prices depend on condition of the surface, access and finish required, so it's designed to gather enough detail for a proper quote or site visit rather than commit to a fixed figure on the spot.

### How is this different from the WhatsApp agent used with Facebook and Instagram adverts?

The qualifying logic is the same, asking about job type, location, timing and fit, but it's placed on your own website instead of following on from an advert lead form. Many plastering businesses run both together so no enquiry, whichever way it arrives, goes unanswered.

### Does it work on mobile?

It needs to, since most people searching for a plasterer in the evening are doing it from their phone. A slow or badly formatted site will lose visitors before the chatbot even gets a chance, which is why it's usually built as part of a proper website rather than added on separately.
