## Why Salford's mix of housing shapes what customers expect

Salford runs from high density apartments at Salford Quays, through older terraced streets in Pendleton and parts of Eccles, out to the more suburban semis and detached houses of Worsley, Swinton and Walkden. Irlam sits slightly apart, with its own mix of housing and a steady trickle of local renovation work.

That spread means your enquiries come from very different places. Rental maintenance and compliance work, re-tiling a bathroom between tenancies, patching a shower enclosure, fitting splashbacks to an agreed spec, is a genuinely large part of the market here, because there are so many landlords and letting agents operating across the city. Those customers want speed, clear pricing structure and proof you can turn jobs around without drama.

At the other end, homeowners in Worsley or Swinton planning a full bathroom or kitchen retile are shopping on trust as much as price. They want to see finished work that looks like their house, not a glossy showroom shot that could be anywhere.

A website that only speaks to one of these groups will quietly lose the other. That's the first thing to check on your own site: does it read as useful to a landlord with three flats near the Quays, and to a family planning one big bathroom in Walkden?

## Websites for tilers in Salford: what makes the difference

The tilers who get consistent enquiries from their website in Salford tend to share a handful of things, and none of them are complicated.

- **Real photos of real jobs, named by area.** A page showing a bathroom retile in Eccles, a kitchen floor in Pendleton and a wet room in Worsley tells a visitor you work locally and you know what their kind of property looks like. Generic stock photography does the opposite.
- **A clear breakdown of services.** Bathroom tiling, kitchen tiling, floor tiling and large format tiles are different jobs with different lead times and skill levels. Listing them separately, rather than one vague 'tiling services' line, helps the right customer find themselves on the page.
- **Fast response built in, not bolted on.** Most tiling enquiries go to whoever replies first with a sensible answer. If your site sends a message to an inbox you check once a day, you are losing jobs to someone who replied in minutes.
- **Simple, visible pricing guidance.** You don't need exact prices, but a rough steer on what a bathroom retile typically involves stops time-wasting enquiries and reassures serious ones.
- **A short, honest about section.** Tiling is a trade where people invite you into their home for several days. A face, a few lines about how you work, and some indication of experience goes further than most tilers assume.

These points apply whether you work solo or run a small team, and they apply whether most of your work currently comes from word of mouth or you're trying to build up enquiries from scratch. ## Where most tiler websites fall short

The most common problem isn't design, it's follow-up. A visitor fills in a contact form on a Tuesday evening after measuring up their bathroom, and hears nothing until Thursday. By then they've messaged two other tilers and booked whoever got back to them first.

The second problem is that many tiler sites don't filter enquiries at all. Every message, whether it's a serious landlord with five flats to maintain or someone asking for a free quote on a single cracked tile, lands in the same inbox and takes the same amount of time to read and reply to.

Both problems have the same fix: respond immediately, and ask the right questions before you ever pick up the phone.
