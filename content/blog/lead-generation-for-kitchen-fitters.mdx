Most kitchen fitters get work in one of three ways: repeat customers, a recommendation from a happy client, or a card left behind after a job. That pipeline works, right up until it doesn't. A quiet spell with no referrals coming through can leave a fitter and their team standing around with nothing booked in for weeks. This is where lead generation for kitchen fitters stops being a marketing buzzword and starts being a practical necessity: a way to bring in enquiries on your own terms, rather than waiting for the phone to ring.

Kitchen work is big-ticket. A full kitchen install can run into several thousand pounds, a worktop replacement is its own smaller job, and a refurbishment (new doors, a re-fit around existing units) sits somewhere in between. Homeowners planning to spend that kind of money tend to get quotes from three or four fitters before deciding, and they usually go with whoever replies first and sounds most organised. If you're not visible online and not quick to respond, you are simply not in that shortlist.

## The trouble with relying on referrals and word of mouth alone

Referrals are the best leads you'll ever get. Someone has seen the finished kitchen, trusts the recommendation, and is already halfway sold before they contact you. The problem is that referrals are unpredictable. They come in bursts after a good job, then dry up for months with no obvious reason. You can't plan a diary of full installs and worktop fits around a stream of enquiries you don't control.

Many kitchen fitters also lean on local Facebook groups and 'trades wanted' posts. These can produce work, but they put you in direct competition with every other fitter who saw the same post, often racing to reply first with a price before anyone has actually visited the property. It rewards speed over quality, and it's exhausting to keep up with on top of running jobs.

A proper lead generation system doesn't replace referrals. It sits alongside them, so a slow month for word of mouth doesn't mean a slow month for the business.

## Lead generation for kitchen fitters: how the system actually works

The mechanics are simpler than they sound, and each step exists to solve a specific problem fitters run into.

1. **Meta ads shown to the right homeowners.** Facebook and Instagram ads run to people within a realistic working radius of your base, not an entire county. The ad shows real finished work: a full kitchen install, a worktop upgrade, a before-and-after refurbishment.
2. **An instant lead form.** Instead of sending people to a website to hunt for a phone number, the ad opens a short form on the spot: name, postcode, rough job type, budget range. Fewer steps means more people actually finish it.
3. **An AI WhatsApp agent replies immediately.** The moment the form is submitted, a message lands on WhatsApp. It asks the follow-up questions that actually matter: is this a full kitchen, a worktop replacement or a refurb, what's the rough budget, and when are they looking to start.
4. **Qualified jobs go straight into your calendar.** Once someone confirms they're in your area, have a sensible budget for the job type, and want to move forward, the booking goes straight into your diary as a call or a site visit. You see it appear rather than having to chase it.

The point of the AI stage is speed and consistency. A homeowner messaging at 9pm on a Sunday still gets an answer straight away, long before you'd normally see the enquiry. ## What a good lead looks like for a kitchen fitting business

Not every enquiry is worth your time, and a decent system should be filtering that out before it reaches you. A strong lead for a full kitchen install typically looks like this: someone within your travel radius, a budget that roughly matches what a full install actually costs, and a timescale of a few months rather than 'just looking'. For a worktop job, it's someone who already has cabinets in place and knows roughly what material they want, quartz, granite or laminate, and is ready to get measured up.

A weak lead looks different: someone 40 miles outside your usual area, a budget well below what any full kitchen would realistically cost, or a message that makes clear they're a year or more away from doing anything. None of that makes them a bad person to talk to eventually, but chasing every one of these by phone is how fitters end up spending evenings on calls that go nowhere.

Good lead generation for kitchen fitters means having those distinctions made automatically, before a lead lands on your calendar, not after you've already driven out to quote.

## Common mistakes that waste ad spend

A few patterns show up repeatedly when kitchen fitters try to run ads themselves or with a generic agency:

- **Targeting too wide an area.** Ads shown across an entire county bring in enquiries you can't realistically travel to, which wastes both ad spend and your time.
- **Using stock photography instead of real work.** Homeowners choosing a kitchen fitter want to see actual finished installs, not generic images that could belong to anyone.
- **No qualifying step at all.** Every enquiry, however unrealistic, ends up as a phone call the fitter has to make personally.
- **Slow response times.** A quote request that sits unanswered for a few hours has often already gone cold, because the homeowner has messaged two or three other fitters in the meantime.
- **Not separating job types.** Full installs, worktops and refurbishments have different budgets and different customers. Treating them as one audience in the ads makes it harder to work out what's actually converting.

A basic website still matters here too. Ads bring people in, but plenty will still look you up before booking a call, so it's worth having a page built for [kitchen fitting businesses specifically](/web-design/kitchens) rather than a generic trades template.

## How to judge whether your lead generation is working

Enquiry volume on its own tells you very little. Twenty enquiries a month sounds good until you realise eighteen of them were unrealistic budgets or people outside your area. The more useful numbers to track are:

- How many enquiries turn into a booked site visit or call, not just a form submission.
- Cost per booked appointment, rather than cost per click or cost per lead.
- How many quotes turn into signed jobs, broken down by full install, worktop and refurb.

Reviewing these weekly, rather than once a quarter, makes it far easier to spot whether a slow patch is a genuine dip in demand or a problem with how leads are being qualified. If cost per booked appointment is climbing steadily, that's worth a proper look before spending more.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does lead generation cost for a kitchen fitting business?

It depends on your working area, how much competition there is locally, and the value of the jobs you're chasing. Rather than guessing, it's more useful to work out your ideal cost per booked appointment against the average value of a full install or worktop job, which is something worth discussing directly through the [contact page](/contact).

### Do I still need a website if I'm running ads?

Yes. Ads and an instant lead form get people talking to you quickly, but many homeowners will still search for your business name before booking a call, so a proper site built around kitchen work, like the approach used for [kitchen websites](/web-design/kitchens), gives them something solid to find.

### What if I don't have time to reply to messages myself?

That's the point of the AI WhatsApp stage. It handles the immediate reply and the qualifying questions around budget, location, job type and timing, so you only pick up the conversation once someone is genuinely worth your time.

### Will this replace referrals and word of mouth?

No, and it shouldn't try to. Referrals are still some of the strongest leads you'll get. This system exists to fill the gaps between them, so a quiet month for recommendations doesn't mean an empty diary.

### How quickly can a new lead generation setup start producing bookings?

Once ads are live and the WhatsApp qualifying is set up, enquiries can start coming in within days, though it usually takes a few weeks of adjusting targeting and messaging before the volume and quality settle into a steady pattern.
