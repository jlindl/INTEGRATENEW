You're halfway up a wall, trowel in hand, grout drying faster than you'd like, and your phone buzzes in your back pocket. By the time you've washed your hands and checked it, the caller has hung up and moved on to the next name on their list. Missed call text back for tilers exists to solve exactly this: a kitchen splashback, a full bathroom retile, or a large format floor job walking straight to a competitor because nobody picked up in time.

It works by replying to every missed call within seconds, on WhatsApp, so the person who called knows you've seen them and haven't just ignored their job.

## Why tilers lose more work to missed calls than other trades

Tiling enquiries tend to arrive in short, decisive bursts. A homeowner has had three quotes for a bathroom refit, they've picked their tiles, and now they're ringing round to see who can start soonest. If the first call goes unanswered, most people don't leave a voicemail and wait patiently. They ring the next number.

This matters more for tilers than for some other trades because the job is rarely urgent in the emergency sense (nobody rings a tiler at 2am), but it is often urgent in the planning sense. The bathroom is already out of action, the kitchen fitter is waiting to hand over, or the floor needs doing before new furniture arrives. Whoever responds first and sounds organised tends to get the job, not necessarily whoever is the best tiler.

You're also usually on site when calls come in. Mixing adhesive, cutting large format porcelain, or working in someone's occupied home means your phone sits in a bag or a van most of the day. That's not a bad habit, it's just the nature of the work. The problem is that homeowners judge your business by how quickly you respond, not by how busy you were when they called.

## How missed call text back actually works

The process is straightforward, and it runs in the background without you having to do anything differently during the working day.

1. A call comes in to your business number and goes unanswered, because you're on a job, driving, or just not near the phone.
2. Within a minute or two, a WhatsApp message goes out automatically to that number. Something like: 'Sorry we missed you, we're on a job at the moment. What do you need doing?'
3. The person replies, usually describing the job: a bathroom retile, a kitchen floor, a shower enclosure. An AI WhatsApp agent picks up the conversation from there.
4. The agent asks a few natural questions to qualify the enquiry: what kind of job it is, roughly when they want it done, where they're based, and whether they have a rough budget or tile choice in mind.
5. Once there's enough information, the agent books the enquiry straight into your calendar, or flags it for you to call back with the details already gathered.

You end up with a diary entry that says something useful, for example 'Mrs Carter, full bathroom retile, 20sqm, wants large format porcelain, available from next Monday, postcode supplied' rather than a missed call notification with no context at all. ## Missed call text back for tilers: what good looks like

A well set up missed call text back system should feel invisible to you and reassuring to the customer, no matter the size of your team or how many jobs you're juggling that week. A few signs it's working properly:

- The reply goes out fast enough that the homeowner hasn't already rung someone else. A few minutes is the difference between keeping the enquiry warm and losing it.
- The message sounds like you, not like a call centre. 'We're on a job at the moment' reads very differently to 'your call is important to us'.
- The questions the AI agent asks are relevant to tiling, not generic. Asking whether it's a wall or floor job, what size the area is, and whether they already have tiles bought makes a real difference to how useful the enquiry is when it lands in your diary.
- Jobs land in your calendar with enough detail that you can prepare, rather than ringing back cold and asking the same questions again.
- You can still see every conversation and take over by text yourself if a particular enquiry needs your personal touch, such as a large commercial job or an awkward access situation.

The aim isn't to replace the conversation you'd have with a customer. It's to make sure that conversation starts within minutes rather than not at all.

## Common mistakes tilers make with missed calls

Most tilers already know missed calls are a problem. Where it tends to go wrong is in how they try to fix it.

**Relying on voicemail.** A voicemail message asking people to leave their name and number filters out a large share of callers, who simply won't bother. Text based replies get answered far more often because they fit how people already communicate.

**Replying hours later.** A text sent back at 6pm after the van's parked up is better than nothing, but by then the homeowner may well have booked someone else. Speed is the whole point.

**Sending a reply that doesn't ask anything.** A text that just says 'sorry we missed your call, we'll ring you back' still leaves the enquiry sitting there with no information attached, and no way of knowing if it's a quick tap repair or a full wet room.

**Treating every enquiry the same.** Not every missed call is a genuine lead. Some are suppliers, some are wrong numbers, some are people getting five quotes with no real intention of booking anyone. A qualifying step that asks about timing, location and job type helps you spot the real opportunities without wasting time chasing them all individually.

**Bolting it onto a website that doesn't match.** If your website looks dated or doesn't explain what you actually do, a fast WhatsApp reply only gets you so far. ## How to judge whether it's actually working

Once it's set up, it's worth checking a few things over the following weeks rather than assuming it's doing its job.

- Compare the number of missed calls against the number of WhatsApp conversations that follow. If a good proportion of missed calls turn into a reply, the system is doing what it should.
- Look at how many of those conversations end with a booked job in the calendar, versus how many go quiet. Conversations that stall often mean the qualifying questions need adjusting, or the first reply isn't landing right.
- Ask new customers, when you're on site, how they found you and whether the quick reply made a difference to who they went with. Their answers tend to tell you more than any dashboard.
- Keep an eye on the type of jobs coming through. If you're getting plenty of small repair enquiries but want more full bathroom and kitchen retiling work, that's a sign to adjust the questions the agent asks, or to look at where the calls are coming from in the first place.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does missed call text back replace answering the phone myself?

No. It's there for the calls you genuinely can't take, whether up a ladder, driving, or mid tile cut. If you can answer, answering in person is always better. The system catches what would otherwise be lost.

### Will customers mind getting a text instead of a call back?

Most people are used to communicating by text and WhatsApp already, and a fast reply tends to be reassuring rather than off-putting. The alternative, silence until you can call back, is what actually puts people off.

### Can the AI WhatsApp agent handle detailed tiling questions?

It's designed to gather the basics (job type, size, location, timing, budget) rather than give technical advice on tile choice or adhesive types. Anything detailed gets flagged for you to pick up directly, with the groundwork already done.

### What happens to enquiries that aren't a good fit, like very small jobs?

The qualifying questions help you see quickly whether an enquiry matches the jobs you actually want. You can still choose whether to follow up on smaller jobs yourself, but at least you're deciding with full information rather than guessing from a missed call log.

### How does this fit with having a website with its own enquiry form?

The same approach can sit on your website too, replying to form submissions and web chat the same way it replies to missed calls, so enquiries get a quick response wherever they come from. The [AI website chatbot](/services/ai-chatbot) covers how that works for web visitors specifically.
