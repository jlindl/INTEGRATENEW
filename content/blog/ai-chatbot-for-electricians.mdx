Someone lands on your website at 9pm after their consumer unit has been tripping all evening. They read your EICR page, think about calling, then notice it's dark outside and decide to wait until morning. By morning they've forgotten, or they've filled in a form on a competitor's site instead. This happens on electricians' websites every single week, and it's one of the quietest ways that good jobs slip through the net. An ai chatbot for electricians is built to stop exactly this.

Most electrician websites are built to be found, not to convert. They have a phone number, a contact form, maybe a WhatsApp link. All of these assume the visitor is willing to wait for a reply, or willing to explain their problem in writing with no guidance. Plenty aren't. They want an answer now, even if it's just confirmation that you do EV charger installs, or a rough idea of what an EICR costs before they commit to anything.

## The problem: enquiries that arrive when nobody's looking

Electrical work tends to generate two kinds of website visitor. The first is urgent: no power to a socket, a tripping breaker, a smell of burning. These people want a phone number and they want it fast. The second is considered: a landlord who needs an EICR before a new tenancy, a homeowner planning an EV charger install, someone pricing up a full rewire before they commit to a renovation. This second group often browses in the evening, after the kids are in bed, or during a lunch break at work, long after you've put the van away.

A standard contact form doesn't help either group much. It asks for a name and a message and then sits there. The visitor has no idea when you'll reply, whether you cover their postcode, or whether the job is even something you handle. Many simply leave and try the next search result instead, and you never know they existed.

Meanwhile, the phone rings during the day while you're up a ladder or elbow deep in a consumer unit. You can't always answer, and a missed call rarely gets followed up with a voicemail, let alone a callback within the hour. The result is the same enquiry lost twice over: once on the website at night, once on the phone during the day.

## How an AI chatbot for electricians works, step by step

An AI chatbot for electricians sits on your own website as a chat widget, available to anyone who visits, at any hour. It works through the same logic as a good office manager would, just without needing a break:

1. A visitor opens the chat and types a question, something like 'do you do EICRs for landlords' or 'how much roughly for an EV charger install'.
2. The assistant answers using information about your business: the jobs you take on, the areas you cover, rough timescales, and anything specific you want it to mention, such as NICEIC or NAPIT registration.
3. It then asks a handful of qualifying questions. For an EICR, that might be the property type, number of bedrooms, and whether it's for a new tenancy or a periodic check. For an EV charger, it might ask about the property's existing supply, whether they own or rent, and where they'd want the charger fitted.
4. Once it has enough detail, it checks your availability and offers a time slot, or passes the enquiry straight into your calendar if the visitor is ready to book.
5. You get a notification with the full conversation, so you walk into the job already knowing what's needed, rather than starting from scratch on the phone.

This is the same approach used by [an AI WhatsApp assistant for joiners](/blog/ai-whatsapp-assistant-for-joiners), applied to your website instead of a phone number. Many electricians run both: a WhatsApp agent for people who find you through Facebook or Instagram, and a website assistant for people who find you through Google or word of mouth. Between them, almost every enquiry gets a reply the moment it arrives, whatever time that is.

## What good looks like

A well set up assistant feels less like a chatbot and more like a knowledgeable receptionist who happens to be awake at 11pm. A few signs it's working properly:

- It gives a straight answer to straight questions, rather than deflecting everything to 'please call us'. If someone asks whether you fit three-phase EV chargers, it should know.
- It asks questions that actually matter for the job, not generic filler. For fault finding, that means asking what's happening (tripping, no power, flickering) rather than just 'what's the issue'.
- It's honest about what it doesn't know. A good assistant says it will check with you and come back, rather than guessing a price for a full rewire on a Victorian terrace with no further detail.
- It books into a calendar that you actually control, so there's no double-booking and no awkward call to say the slot's gone.
- It hands over cleanly. Once a job is booked or the enquiry is too complex for chat, it should offer a direct way to reach you, not trap the visitor in a loop.

The test is simple: would you be comfortable if this conversation happened with a customer standing in front of you? If the answer gives the same information you'd give yourself, it's doing its job.

## Common mistakes electricians make with chatbots

The biggest mistake is installing a generic chatbot and leaving it on the default settings. Off-the-shelf widgets often can't answer anything specific to electrical work, so they end up repeating 'please leave your details' regardless of what's typed, which frustrates visitors more than a plain contact form would.

Another common issue is setting it up once and never updating it. If you stop offering a service, change your coverage area, or your lead times for a rewire shift from two weeks to six, the assistant needs to know. An assistant working from outdated information will happily book jobs you can't deliver on.

Some electricians also use the assistant to try to close every enquiry on price. For simple things like a lighting fitting or a socket swap, a rough estimate is reasonable. For an EICR on a large HMO or a full rewire, quoting a fixed price without seeing the property undersells the job and sets the wrong expectation before you've even walked through the door. The assistant should gather detail and offer a site visit or a call back for anything that genuinely needs eyes on the property.

Finally, some businesses bolt the assistant onto a website that wasn't built to support it, a slow, cluttered site where the chat widget is the only modern thing on the page. Visitors notice the mismatch. ## Judging whether it's actually working

Once it's live, judge it the same way you'd judge a new member of staff: by results, not by how clever it sounds.

Track how many conversations turn into booked calls or site visits, not just how many messages come in. A high number of chats with a low number of bookings usually means the qualifying questions need adjusting, or the assistant is being too vague on pricing and timescales.

Check the conversations themselves every so often. Read through a handful each month. Look for questions it couldn't answer well, and feed those answers back in. This is the single most useful habit for keeping it accurate, especially as your services or pricing shift.

Compare enquiries before and after it went live, particularly ones arriving outside your normal hours. If evening and weekend bookings start showing up on your calendar that wouldn't have reached you otherwise, it's paying for itself. ## Frequently asked questions

### Will an AI chatbot replace my receptionist or office manager?

No. It's built to catch the enquiries that currently get missed, mainly evenings, weekends and moments when you can't get to the phone. Anything complex still gets passed to you or your team to handle properly.

### Can it quote accurately for jobs like rewires or consumer unit upgrades?

It can give a sensible range based on property type and rough scope, but jobs like full rewires genuinely need a site visit before a firm price. A good assistant gathers detail and books that visit rather than guessing a number it can't stand behind.

### Does it work alongside a WhatsApp assistant, or instead of one?

Both, usually. The website assistant catches visitors who find you through search or recommendation, while a WhatsApp agent handles enquiries that come through ads or direct messages. Running them together means fewer gaps.

### How much maintenance does it need once it's set up?

Not much, but it isn't a 'set and forget' tool either. Update it whenever your services, coverage area or lead times change, and check through real conversations occasionally to spot gaps in what it knows.

### Is this only useful for businesses that are already busy?

No, it helps at any stage. For a quieter business it means fewer enquiries slip away while you're building up work. For a busy one it means less time spent on the phone repeating the same basic questions to every caller.
