If you run a painting and decorating business in Wigan, you already know the work is out there. The question is whether it finds you before it finds one of the other decorators working the same postcodes. Marketing for painters and decorators in Wigan isn't about clever slogans or a bigger van sign. It's about being visible to the right households at the right moment, and being quick enough to pick up the enquiry before someone else does.

Wigan's housing stock makes this a bit more interesting than in some towns. You've got dense terraced streets close to the centre, many of them Victorian or early twentieth century, needing careful prep work and old plaster sorting out. Then you've got the semis and newer estates further out in Standish and Orrell, where jobs tend to be fuller redecorations or exterior work on render and UPVC trims. A decorator who understands both markets, and can show it, has an edge over one who just lists 'interior and exterior painting' and leaves it there.

## Why marketing for painters and decorators in Wigan looks different to other towns

Wigan sits close to the M6, M58 and M61, so decorators here aren't boxed into one small catchment. Plenty of local firms cover the whole borough, taking on work in Hindley, Ashton-in-Makerfield and Shevington, and pushing further into Leigh, St Helens or Chorley when things are quiet closer to home. That's sensible, but it means your marketing has to work across a wider area without becoming vague. 'Decorator covering Wigan and surrounding areas' tells a homeowner in Standish almost nothing about whether you actually know their road.

The other local factor is competition. Painting and decorating has a low barrier to entry, and Wigan has no shortage of sole traders and small teams chasing the same jobs. Word of mouth still matters, but it's slow, and it dries up the moment you're between recommendations. Relying on it alone leaves gaps in the diary that referrals won't fill fast enough.

## What actually brings in enquiries

Most decorators in and around Wigan get work from a mix of these, usually without much structure:

- Word of mouth and repeat customers
- A Facebook or Instagram page with before and after photos
- A listing on a trade directory
- A basic website, if they have one at all

The issue isn't that these don't work. It's that none of them are consistent, and none of them put you in front of someone in Standish or Orrell who's thinking about redecorating right now but hasn't searched for a decorator yet. That's where paid social advertising changes the picture. Meta ads, run specifically to people in Wigan and the nearby towns you cover, put your work in front of homeowners while they're scrolling, not just when they happen to search.

A good ad for a decorator doesn't need to be flashy. A short clip of a room transformation, or a clear before and after photo, with a simple offer to get a free quote, tends to outperform anything trying too hard to sound professional. The ad links to an instant lead form rather than sending people off to search for your number, because the easier you make it to enquire, the more enquiries you get. ## The part most decorators get wrong: speed of reply

Here's the thing that actually costs decorators jobs in Wigan. A homeowner fills in a form at half seven in the evening, after the kids are in bed and they've finally had five minutes to think about getting the hallway done. If nobody replies until the next afternoon, they've often already messaged two other decorators, and whoever answers first tends to get the job, regardless of who's better at the work.

This is solvable without you sitting on your phone all evening. An AI WhatsApp assistant can reply the moment someone enquires, ask the questions you'd normally ask anyway (what rooms, what size, roughly when, any wallpaper to strip, rough budget) and then book a suitable time straight into your calendar. You get a qualified lead with the details already gathered, not a one-line message asking 'how much to paint a room' with no context. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a decorator in Wigan spend on advertising?
There's no fixed figure that suits everyone, because it depends on how many extra jobs a month you want and what an average job is worth to you. The sensible approach is to start with a modest, controlled budget targeted tightly at Wigan and the nearby areas you cover, see what it brings in, and adjust from there rather than guessing a number upfront.

### Is Facebook advertising actually worth it for a small decorating business?
For local trades, Facebook and Instagram ads work well because they let you target people by location and interests rather than relying on them to search for you. For a decorator covering a specific area like Wigan, Standish and Orrell, that targeting is a genuine advantage over directory listings that anyone in the country can appear on.

### Do I need a website if I already get work through Facebook?
A Facebook page helps, but a website gives you a permanent, organised place to show your work and gives homeowners confidence before they call, particularly for larger jobs. It also protects you from relying entirely on one platform's algorithm.

### What areas should I mention in my marketing if I'm based in Wigan?
Be specific rather than vague. Naming the towns you genuinely cover, such as Hindley, Ashton-in-Makerfield, Shevington or Leigh, tells potential customers you know the area rather than operating a postcode radius you've never thought about.

### How quickly do I need to respond to enquiries to win the job?
As close to immediately as possible. Many homeowners message more than one decorator when they're ready to get quotes, and the first sensible reply often has the advantage, which is why an automated first response matters even before you've had chance to pick up the phone.
