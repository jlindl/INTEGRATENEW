A loft conversion is not a job most homeowners decide on a whim. They have usually been thinking about it for weeks or months, measuring the roof space, checking whether a dormer or a Velux conversion suits the house, wondering what it might cost. Facebook ads for loft conversion specialists work well because they catch people at exactly that stage: browsing on a phone in the evening, weighing up options, not yet ready to ring round for quotes but happy to fill in a short form if it feels low commitment.

The trouble is most loft conversion businesses either rely on word of mouth and referrals, which is steady but slow, or they run generic Facebook ads that point at a website and hope for the best. A website enquiry form on its own loses people: they get distracted, close the tab, or type half a message and give up. What actually works is combining local targeting with an instant lead form and fast, useful follow up, so an enquiry about a dormer conversion on a three bed semi arrives with enough detail to act on before the lead goes cold.

## Why loft conversions need a different approach to marketing

Loft conversions sit in an odd spot for advertising. The average job is worth many thousands of pounds, so nobody signs off on it after seeing one advert. But the trade is also full of smaller jobs, loft boarding for storage, a new staircase, a Velux window replacing an old one, that get booked far more quickly.

A generic 'get a quote' Facebook ad sent to a homepage does not account for any of this. It treats a homeowner asking about boarding a loft for Christmas storage the same way as a family planning a full dormer extension with a bedroom and en suite. Both might click the same ad, but they need different questions asked and different handling once they enquire.

This is where targeting by service area and job type, feeding into a form built for the trade, makes the difference. It is the same principle covered in [lead generation for kitchen fitters](/blog/lead-generation-for-kitchen-fitters): match the ad and the form to the actual job, not a general enquiry.

## Facebook ads for loft conversion specialists: how the setup actually works

The mechanics matter more than the creative. A campaign set up properly for a loft conversion business usually works like this:

1. The ad targets a radius around the areas the business actually travels to for surveys and builds, rather than an entire county. Postcodes with a high proportion of bungalows, terraces with no usable roof void, or flats can be excluded, since they are unlikely ever to need a loft conversion.
2. The advert shows real finished work: a dormer conversion with the new roofline visible, a Velux installation letting light into an existing loft, a staircase fitted into a tight landing. Photos of an actual outcome do more work than any slogan.
3. Instead of linking out to a website, the ad opens an instant form on Facebook or Instagram itself. This matters because every extra tap or page load loses people, and mobile users in particular drop out of multi page website forms.
4. The form asks a handful of specific questions: what type of conversion they are considering (dormer, Velux, hip to gable, or just boarding and a loft ladder), roughly when they want the work done, and their postcode.
5. As soon as the form is submitted, an AI assistant on WhatsApp messages the homeowner, confirms the details, fills any gaps, and offers times for a survey. It works the same way as the [AI WhatsApp assistant built for joiners](/blog/ai-whatsapp-assistant-for-joiners), reading what the customer has already told you and asking sensible follow up questions rather than sending a generic 'someone will call you back'.
6. A survey slot lands straight in the business owner's calendar, with the homeowner's answers attached, so there is no guessing what they want before the visit.

Set up this way, a loft conversion specialist is not chasing a list of half finished enquiries at the end of the week. They are looking at a diary of surveys, each one already telling them what type of job it is and what the customer has in mind.

## What a good result looks like in practice

The clearest sign this is working is not the number of leads, it is the quality of the conversation once a lead comes in.

For a dormer conversion enquiry, a good lead arrives with the postcode, an idea of what the loft will be used for (an extra bedroom, an office, a bathroom), and a rough timescale. That is enough to book a survey without a back and forth of text messages first.

For loft boarding, volume is usually higher and value per job lower, so speed matters more than detail. A homeowner who fills in a form on a Tuesday evening wanting boarding done before winter should hear back that same evening, not three days later when they have already booked someone else.

For a staircase replacement as part of a wider conversion, the useful detail is the current layout: is it a straight run, a winder, how much headroom is there. Ads and forms aimed at this job type should ask about the existing stairs directly, rather than relying on a 'tell us about your project' box that most people leave blank or fill with one line.

Across all of these, the test is the same: does the enquiry give the business owner enough to have a proper first conversation, rather than starting from 'so, what were you thinking of doing to your loft?'

## Common mistakes loft conversion specialists make with Facebook ads

A few patterns come up again and again:

- **Targeting too wide.** A radius covering an entire region brings in leads from areas the business will not realistically travel to for a survey, let alone a build.
- **Sending clicks to a homepage.** A generic website, however nice it looks, asks a stranger to hunt for a contact form. An instant lead form removes that friction.
- **Asking one vague question.** A single 'describe your project' box gets skipped or filled with almost nothing useful. Specific questions about job type, budget bracket and timing get specific answers.
- **Slow follow up.** Loft conversion enquiries are considered purchases, but that does not mean people are patient once they have decided to ask. A lead left for two days often books with whoever replied first.
- **Using stock photography.** Homeowners researching a dormer or Velux conversion have usually looked at several companies already. Real photos of actual jobs, including the less glamorous ones like loft boarding, build more trust than polished stock images.
- **No way to filter out non-jobs.** Without qualifying questions, ads also attract people asking about attic ladders for a cupboard or general roof repairs, which is not what the campaign is meant to bring in.

Fixing most of these is less about spending more on ads and more about tightening the targeting, the questions on the form, and how quickly someone hears back.

## How to judge whether the campaign is working

Ignore likes and clicks. For a loft conversion business, three numbers matter more:

- **Cost per qualified lead**, not cost per click. A cheap click that never turns into a real enquiry is not worth anything.
- **Show up rate for surveys.** If people are booking survey slots and then not answering the door, leads are being generated but not properly qualified before the visit.
- **The ratio of enquiries to signed jobs**, tracked by job type. Dormer conversions, Velux jobs and boarding will convert at different rates, and knowing which performs best tells you where to spend more.

Give a campaign a few weeks rather than a few days before judging it. Loft conversion enquiries do not all arrive on the day someone first sees the ad, and it takes a run of surveys to see which job types are converting well.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to run Facebook ads for loft conversion specialists?

Costs vary depending on how densely populated the target area is and how many other loft conversion firms are already advertising there. Rather than fixing a set budget in advance, it is more useful to track cost per qualified lead once the campaign is live and adjust from there.

### Do Instagram ads work as well as Facebook ads for this trade?

Both run through the same platform and targeting, so it is less a choice between them and more about using both. Instagram tends to suit photo led before and after content well, while Facebook usually reaches a slightly older homeowner audience and often brings more volume for this trade.

### What is the best time of year to run loft conversion ads?

Enquiries often pick up early in the year when people are planning home improvements, and again before winter for jobs like boarding that people want finished before the cold weather. Running ads steadily through the year tends to work better than starting and stopping.

### Should the ads link to a website or straight to a lead form?

An instant form built into Facebook or Instagram itself usually performs better than sending clicks to a website, since it removes the extra step of loading a page and hunting for a contact form. A website still matters for people who search for the business directly, just not as the landing point for the ad click.

### Can this work alongside a website and referrals?

Yes. Paid ads bring in a steady stream of new enquiries from people who were not actively searching yet, while referrals and a good website continue to bring in people who already have the business in mind. Running them together, rather than relying on just one, tends to keep the diary fuller through quieter months.
