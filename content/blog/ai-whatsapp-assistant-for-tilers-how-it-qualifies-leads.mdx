If you fit tiles for a living, the enquiry that lands at half nine on a Sunday night is not a nuisance, it is a potential bathroom or kitchen job worth several thousand pounds. An AI WhatsApp assistant for tilers exists to deal with exactly that moment. It replies in seconds, asks the questions you would ask on the phone, and only puts the real jobs in your diary.

Most tilers lose work not because the quote was too high, but because nobody replied quickly enough. The homeowner messages three tilers from a Facebook ad or Google search, and books in with whoever answers first with something sensible. If you are up a ladder, under a bath, or elbow deep in adhesive when that message comes through, you are already behind.

## Why tiling enquiries fall through the cracks

Tiling work has a particular rhythm. A bathroom retile or a kitchen floor is rarely an emergency, so people enquire in the evening, at the weekend, or on their lunch break, often to several trades at once. They are comparing prices, availability and how quickly someone bothers to reply.

The problem is not a lack of enquiries. Most tilers running Facebook ads or a decent website get a steady trickle of messages. The problem is what happens in the gap between the enquiry landing and someone actually replying with useful questions.

That gap causes three specific problems for tiling businesses:

- Good leads go cold because a competitor answered faster, even with a less polished quote.
- Poor fits waste your evening. Someone wants 2 square metres of splashback tiled for £80, or lives two hours away, and you only find out after a back and forth of messages.
- You end up doing the admin at 9pm instead of with your family, scrolling through Facebook messages, texts and missed calls trying to work out who is worth calling back.

An AI WhatsApp assistant removes that gap. It does not replace you making the final decision on a job. It replaces the slow, manual sorting that happens before you ever see a serious enquiry.

## How an AI WhatsApp assistant for tilers works, step by step

The setup is simpler than it sounds, and it slots in around how tiling leads already arrive.

1. A homeowner sees a Facebook or Instagram ad, or lands on your website, and fills in a short enquiry form, or messages you directly.
2. Within moments, an AI agent messages them on WhatsApp, introducing itself as working with your business and picking up the conversation straight away.
3. The agent asks the questions that actually matter for a tiling job: what room (bathroom, kitchen, floor, hallway), roughly how much tiling is involved, whether tiles are already bought or need sourcing, the postcode, and when they want the work done.
4. It asks about budget in a natural way, so a job with an unrealistic budget (someone expecting a full bathroom retile for a few hundred pounds, for instance) gets flagged early rather than after you have driven out to quote.
5. If the job fits what you take on, the assistant books a slot straight into your calendar, whether that is a call, a site visit, or a provisional quote date.
6. If it is not a fit, perhaps the location is too far, or the job is too small to be worth the trip, the assistant lets the person down politely rather than leaving them on read.

You see the conversation as it happens, and you can step in at any point. Nothing gets booked without the information you need to turn up prepared, knowing whether it is a full bathroom suite, a kitchen floor in large format porcelain, or a small splashback.

Job type, location and budget mean something different for a tiler than they do for a joiner fitting a staircase, so the questions the assistant asks need to reflect that.

## What good looks like

A well set up assistant feels like a helpful member of staff, not a chatbot bouncing out scripted replies. For a tiling business, that means it understands the difference between a straightforward floor tiling job and a bathroom that needs tanking, levelling and a new shower tray before a single tile goes down.

Good examples of what it should catch and sort:

- A homeowner wants large format porcelain tiles laid on an uneven kitchen floor. The assistant asks about the current floor and flags that this is a bigger job than it first sounds, so you are not caught out on site.
- An enquiry comes in for a small ensuite retile with a tight deadline before a house sale. The assistant confirms the timescale and location, and books it in straight away because it is a good fit.
- Someone wants tiling done but has not bought tiles yet and has no real budget in mind. The assistant gently establishes a ballpark figure so you are not quoting blind.
- A message comes from 40 miles outside your usual area for a small job. The assistant explains your coverage area rather than booking something that was never going to be worth the diesel.

The result is a diary that fills up with jobs you actually want, rather than a phone full of messages you have to sift through after a long day.

## Common mistakes to avoid

The businesses that get the least from this kind of setup usually make one of a few mistakes.

The first is treating it as a one off task and never reviewing the questions it asks. Tiling businesses evolve, some take on more large format and wetroom work, others move away from small jobs entirely. The assistant's questions should move with you.

The second is being too slow to follow up once a job is booked in. The assistant does the qualifying and the booking, but it is still you, or your team, who needs to turn up to the quote or call at the agreed time. A booked slot that gets missed undoes all the good work of a fast reply.

The third is being too rigid about what counts as a good fit. If the filters are too tight, you will turn away decent jobs that do not match the exact criteria. It is worth loosening things occasionally and reviewing what came through, especially in quieter months.

The fourth is forgetting that this only works well alongside a steady flow of enquiries in the first place. An assistant that qualifies leads brilliantly is no use if there are only two enquiries a month to work with. ## How to judge whether it is working

You do not need complicated reporting to know if an AI WhatsApp assistant is earning its keep. A few practical signs are enough.

- Response time: are enquiries getting a reply within a minute or two, at any time of day, rather than hours later?
- Show up rate: are the quotes and site visits in your diary turning into real conversations, or are a lot of people not answering when you call?
- Job quality: are the jobs coming through broadly in line with what you want to be doing, bathroom retiles, kitchen floors, large format work, rather than a string of small jobs you would rather not take on?
- Your own time: are you spending less of your evening replying to messages and more of it actually off the clock?

If those answers are trending the right way after a few weeks, it is doing its job. If not, it is usually a sign the questions it asks need adjusting rather than a sign the idea does not work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the AI assistant replace me quoting the job myself?

No. It handles the initial reply, the questions and the booking. You still visit, measure up and give the final price, the same as you would now, just with better information before you arrive.

### What if a lead asks something the assistant cannot answer?

It is built to hand over naturally, either by flagging the conversation for you to step in, or by letting the person know you will be in touch directly. It is not designed to pretend it knows things it does not.

### Can it work alongside enquiries that come through Facebook ads rather than a website form?

Yes. It is commonly set up to pick up leads from Meta lead ads directly on WhatsApp, which suits tiling businesses that rely on local Facebook and Instagram advertising for most of their enquiries.

### Will it put off homeowners who want to speak to a real person?

Most people are comfortable messaging on WhatsApp, and the assistant is upfront about what it is. If someone clearly wants a phone call instead, that preference gets passed straight to you.

### How long does it take to set up for a tiling business?

It depends on how your enquiries currently arrive and what questions you want asked, but it is generally a short project rather than a long one, since the questions a tiler needs answering (room, size, budget, timing, location) are well established.
