If you run a painting and decorating business, you already know where most of your enquiries go wrong. Someone fills in a form or messages your Facebook page about a kitchen repaint, and by the time you've finished a job, cleaned your brushes and checked your phone, they've already booked someone else. An AI WhatsApp assistant for painters and decorators exists to close that gap. It replies the moment an enquiry comes in, asks the questions you'd ask anyway, and only puts the good jobs in your diary.

This isn't about replacing the conversation with a customer. It's about making sure that conversation starts within moments rather than hours, and that by the time you do pick up the phone, you already know whether it's a two-day wallpapering job in a terraced hallway or a three-storey exterior repaint that needs scaffolding.

## The problem painters and decorators actually have

Most decorators aren't short of enquiries. They're short of time to deal with them properly. A typical week might include a full day up a ladder on an exterior job, an afternoon quoting for a commercial unit, and evenings spent trying to reply to messages that came in while you were working. Enquiries arrive at odd times: a homeowner browsing at 9pm after putting the kids to bed, a landlord messaging between viewings, a shop owner who wants a quote before they open at 8am.

Every hour that passes between an enquiry landing and someone replying is an hour where the homeowner is free to message the next decorator on the list. Painting and decorating is a trade where people often get three or four quotes before deciding, so speed matters as much as price.

There's a second problem underneath the first one: not every enquiry is worth your time. Some people want a single wall touched up for £80. Some are in a postcode an hour's drive away. Some want the job done by next Tuesday when your diary is full until the month after. Without a system in place, you end up either ignoring messages (and losing good leads along with the bad ones) or replying to everyone individually, which eats into the hours you should be spending on paintwork.

## How an AI WhatsApp assistant for painters and decorators actually works

The setup is straightforward, and it sits on top of however people are already finding you, whether that's a Facebook or Instagram ad, your website, or a shared number on a van sign.

1. A lead comes in, usually through a short enquiry form attached to an ad or a website contact page.
2. Within moments, an AI agent messages them on WhatsApp, a channel most homeowners already use and check far more often than email.
3. The agent asks the questions that actually determine whether a job is right for you: what kind of work it is (interior decorating, exterior painting, wallpapering, commercial), roughly what budget they have in mind, where the property is, and when they'd want the work done.
4. If the answers line up with the jobs you want, the agent books the enquiry straight into your calendar, ready for you to confirm or go and quote.
5. If the answers don't line up, say the property is 40 miles outside your area, or the budget is clearly too low for the scope described, the lead is filtered out rather than left sitting in your inbox waiting for a reply you were never going to have time to send.

The questions can be tuned to how your business actually works. A decorator who mostly does interior work for homeowners will want different qualifying questions to one who does mainly commercial fit-outs for landlords and letting agents. An exterior painting job depends heavily on access and the condition of the surface, so those details can be asked up front rather than discovered on the day of the quote.

The same approach can sit on your website too, so a visitor browsing your gallery of finished rooms at 10pm can start a conversation and get answers immediately, rather than filling in a form and waiting until the next working day.

## What good looks like in practice

A well set up assistant should feel invisible to the customer in the best sense: they get a quick, helpful reply, a few relevant questions, and a confirmed appointment or call back time, without ever feeling like they're talking to a script. Someone asking about wallpapering a living room should be asked about room size, whether they're supplying the paper, and when they'd like it done, not generic questions that don't apply to their job.

For you, good looks like opening your calendar in the morning and seeing jobs already booked in, with the key details attached: job type, rough budget, location and timing, all gathered before you've had your first coffee. It means the enquiries that reach your phone directly are the ones worth your time, rather than a mixed bag of tyre kickers and genuine prospects you have to sort through yourself.

It also means fewer wasted callouts. If someone wants an exterior repaint on a Victorian terrace with render in poor condition, finding that out by message before you quote saves a wasted trip, or at least means you arrive prepared rather than surprised.

## Common mistakes to avoid

The businesses that get the least out of this kind of setup usually fall into one of a few traps:

- Treating it as a chatbot for everything. The assistant should handle enquiry and booking, not try to answer every detailed question about paint brands or finishes. Those conversations still belong with you.
- Asking too many questions. Four or five qualifying questions is plenty. Push past that and people stop replying, which defeats the point.
- Never reviewing the filtered leads. The criteria for a good job can drift over time, especially if you take on more commercial work or expand into a wider area. Check periodically that the filters still match the jobs you actually want.
- Forgetting the human handover. The moment a job is qualified and booked, someone still needs to confirm it, visit, and quote. The assistant removes the chasing, not the decorating.

## How to judge whether it's working

The honest way to tell if an AI WhatsApp assistant is earning its keep is to track a few simple things over a month or two:

- How quickly enquiries get a first reply, compared with before.
- The proportion of enquiries that turn into a booked quote or appointment.
- How many of those booked appointments turn out to be a genuine fit, rather than a mismatch on budget or location that should have been filtered out.
- Whether you're spending less time replying to messages in the evenings and at weekends.

If the filtered-out leads are consistently jobs you'd have taken anyway, the questions need adjusting. If the booked jobs are consistently a good match for the work you want, it's doing its job. The two work best together: a site and ads that bring enquiries in, and an assistant that makes sure none of them go cold.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will customers know they're messaging an AI rather than a person?

The conversation is designed to be clear and natural rather than deceptive. Most homeowners care more about getting a quick, useful answer than about who or what sent it. If a question comes up that needs a real person, the conversation hands over smoothly.

### What happens to the enquiries that get filtered out?

They're not deleted. You still get visibility of them, so you can follow up yourself if you want to, for instance if someone's budget was slightly under what you'd normally take but the job is otherwise appealing.

### Does this work for commercial decorating enquiries as well as domestic?

Yes, the qualifying questions are set up around the type of work you do, so a business asking about repainting a shop front or office can be asked about access, working hours and timescale, which matter more for commercial jobs than for a single room repaint.

### How long does it take to set up?

Most of the work is in defining the right questions and the criteria for a good job, which is a conversation rather than a technical task. Once that's agreed, the assistant can be connected to your existing enquiry sources fairly quickly.

### Can it replace having a website or Facebook ads altogether?

No. It qualifies and books the enquiries you're already getting; it doesn't generate them on its own. It works alongside your existing marketing, whether that's a website, Meta ads, or word of mouth, rather than instead of it.
