A loft conversion is one of the biggest jobs a homeowner will book in their lifetime, and most of them will only ever do it once. That makes working out how to get more loft conversion leads a different problem to chasing enquiries for a boiler service or a gutter repair. You are not selling to a crowd of repeat customers. You are reaching a smaller pool of homeowners who need convincing, reassuring and then handing a diary slot before they drift off to the next specialist they find.

Most loft conversion businesses still rely heavily on word of mouth, a handful of referrals and a website that gets looked at once a homeowner has already decided to get three quotes. That works, until the phone goes quiet for a fortnight and there is no pipeline behind it. This guide covers how the problem shows up specifically for loft conversion specialists, how a proper lead generation system handles it, and how to tell if it is actually working.

## Why loft conversion leads behave differently to other trade enquiries

A dormer conversion, a Velux installation, a staircase relocation and a straightforward loft boarding job are not the same enquiry, even though they all start with someone typing 'loft conversion' into Facebook or Google. The price points are miles apart, the decision-making timeline is miles apart, and the questions in a homeowner's head are different for each one.

Someone asking about loft boarding usually wants extra storage, has a modest budget, and will often book the first tradesperson who replies. Someone asking about a full dormer conversion is thinking about planning permission, party wall agreements if they live in a terrace or semi, building control sign-off, scaffolding access, and whether their roof pitch and head height will even support the work. They will compare several specialists over several weeks before committing, and they expect to be taken seriously on the first message.

That gap is where leads get lost. A generic enquiry form cannot tell the difference between a £1,500 boarding job and a £45,000 dormer with a new staircase, so every enquiry lands in the same inbox looking identical. Without a way to separate them quickly, you end up either spending an hour on the phone with someone who was never going to proceed, or missing a genuine dormer enquiry because it got buried under three boarding questions.

## How to get more loft conversion leads: what good looks like

When this is set up properly, the difference is not just more enquiries, it is better ones. A few signs it is working as it should:

- You get a reply out to every enquiry within minutes, not hours, so homeowners who are comparing three specialists hear from you first.
- By the time you see the lead, you already know whether it is a dormer conversion, a Velux job, loft boarding or a staircase enquiry, along with the rough budget and timeframe.
- Your site visits are booked, not just promised. A name and number in a spreadsheet is not the same as a confirmed slot in your calendar.
- The mix of enquiries matches what you actually want to be doing. If you have moved away from small boarding jobs to focus on full dormer conversions, the targeting and the AI agent's questions should reflect that.
- You spend less time on enquiries that were never going anywhere, because unrealistic budgets, out-of-area postcodes and the wrong job type get filtered out before they reach your diary.

## Common mistakes that cost loft conversion specialists enquiries

A few patterns come up again and again in this trade specifically.

**Replying too slowly.** A homeowner looking into a dormer conversion has usually messaged two or three specialists on the same day. Whoever replies first with something useful often gets the site visit, regardless of who ends up doing the best job.

**Treating every enquiry the same.** Sending the same follow-up message to someone asking about loft boarding and someone asking about a full conversion with a new staircase wastes everyone's time. The questions, and the urgency, should differ.

**Targeting too wide an area.** Advertising across an entire county rather than the towns and postcodes where the housing stock actually suits conversion work means paying for clicks from people whose roof will never support a dormer in the first place.

**No qualification before the site visit.** Driving out to quote a job that turns out to have no planning route, an unsuitable roof pitch, or a budget a tenth of what the work costs is a wasted morning. A few questions upfront, asked by an AI agent rather than you, avoids most of that.

**Letting the website gather dust.** If your site still shows the same three photos from years ago with no mention of building regs, planning, or recent dormer work, it does little to reassure a homeower mid-comparison. ## How to judge whether your lead generation is actually working

Enquiry count on its own tells you very little. Ten enquiries that go nowhere are worth less than three that turn into booked surveys. A few things worth tracking instead:

- **Cost per qualified lead**, not just cost per enquiry. A cheap lead that was never in your area or budget range is not actually cheap.
- **Site surveys booked per week**, since that is the step that actually moves a job towards being signed.
- **The ratio of surveys to signed jobs.** If this is low, the problem may not be lead volume at all, it may be qualification, pricing, or how the survey itself is handled.
- **Response time.** If messages are sitting unanswered overnight, that alone can account for a chunk of lost work regardless of how good the ads are.
- **Where jobs actually come from**, whether that is loft boarding, Velux conversions or full dormers with a staircase, so you can see whether your marketing is attracting the work you want more of.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many leads does a loft conversion business actually need each month?

Far fewer than most trades, because the jobs are larger and less frequent. A handful of well-qualified enquiries that turn into booked site surveys is usually worth more than a high volume of unfiltered ones, particularly for full dormer conversions.

### Will Facebook ads work for a job as expensive as a loft conversion?

Yes, though the targeting needs to be tighter than for lower-cost trades. Ads work best when shown to homeowners in areas with suitable housing stock, paired with photos of real conversions rather than stock imagery, since trust matters more at this price point.

### Can the AI WhatsApp agent tell the difference between a boarding job and a full conversion?

Yes. The qualifying questions are built around your typical jobs, so it asks different follow-up questions depending on whether someone mentions loft boarding, a Velux installation or a dormer conversion with a new staircase, then books the right type of appointment.

### Do I need a new website before I start running ads?

Not always, but it helps. If your current site has no recent photos, no mention of planning or building regs, and no way to book a survey directly, it will undercut the ads sending traffic to it. A focused rebuild through [websites for trades](/services/websites-for-trades) is often worth doing alongside the ad campaign rather than after it.

### How quickly should I expect to see results?

Ads and the lead form can start producing enquiries within days of going live. What takes a little longer is tuning the targeting and the qualifying questions so the leads coming through consistently match the jobs you actually want, which usually settles down over the first few weeks.
