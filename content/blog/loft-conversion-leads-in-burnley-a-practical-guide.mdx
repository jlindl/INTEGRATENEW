## Why Burnley is a different market to selling lofts elsewhere

Most of the housing stock across Burnley, Nelson and Colne is Victorian stone terrace, often two up two down with a cellar and a shallow roof pitch. That changes the conversation with a customer. A dormer conversion on a stone terrace usually needs more structural thought than the same job on a 1990s semi: party wall considerations with neighbours sharing the roof line, older timber that may need sistering or replacing, and roof coverings that have to tie in with slate or stone flags rather than standard tiles.

Customers here are also price-conscious, and they've usually asked around before they contact you. Wet Pennine weather means roofs, gutters and pointing are already on people's minds, so a conversation about a loft often starts with a worry about damp rather than an extra bedroom. That gives you an opening: if you can explain clearly how a conversion deals with insulation, ventilation and moisture in an old stone building, you sound like someone who understands the property, not just someone quoting a price off a spreadsheet.

## Why word of mouth and a basic website aren't enough anymore

Most loft conversion businesses working around Burnley still rely heavily on recommendations and a Facebook page updated now and again. That works, up to a point. The problem is it only reaches people who already know you exist, and it does nothing for the homeowner in Hapton or Worsthorne who's just started thinking about a conversion and is scrolling their phone in the evening, not searching for you by name.

A proper system does three things word of mouth can't:

- It puts your business in front of homeowners in Burnley and the surrounding areas who fit your ideal job, before they've decided who to call.
- It replies to an enquiry in minutes, not the next morning, which matters because the first tradesperson to respond usually gets the conversation.
- It filters out people who aren't realistic about budget or timescale, so you spend your evenings talking to people who are ready to book, not chasing dead ends.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a dormer loft conversion typically cost in Burnley?

Costs vary a lot depending on the size of the property, the roof structure and whether structural work like steel beams is needed, so it's not something to quote without seeing the property. What matters more for winning the job is explaining clearly, early on, what's included and why older stone terraces in Burnley often need extra structural consideration.

### Do I need planning permission for a loft conversion in Burnley?

Many loft conversions fall under permitted development, but this depends on the size of the extension, the roof style and whether the property is in a conservation area. Parts of Burnley, including areas near the Weavers' Triangle, have conservation considerations, so it's worth checking with the local planning department before quoting a firm timeline.

### How quickly should I reply to a loft conversion enquiry?

As fast as possible. Homeowners researching loft conversions usually message more than one tradesperson at the same time, and the first one to respond with a clear, useful answer often gets the booking. An automated first reply within minutes, followed by a real conversation, keeps you in the running even when you're on site all day.

### Is Facebook advertising worth it for a small loft conversion business?

It can be, if the ads are targeted tightly to homeowners in the right areas rather than run broadly across a wide region. For a trade with a high job value like loft conversions, even a modest number of well-qualified enquiries a month can be worth the spend, especially compared with chasing unqualified leads.

### Can this approach work alongside recommendations and existing customers?

Yes. Most loft conversion specialists in and around Burnley still get strong work from past customers and word of mouth. A structured lead generation setup runs alongside that, filling the quieter periods and reaching homeowners who haven't heard of you yet, rather than replacing the referrals you already rely on.
