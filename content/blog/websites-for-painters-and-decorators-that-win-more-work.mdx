Most painters and decorators get work through word of mouth, referrals and repeat customers. That's fine until the phone goes quiet for a fortnight and you need new work fast. At that point, the first thing a homeowner does is search online, and what they find decides whether you get the enquiry or your competitor down the road does. Websites for painters and decorators have a specific job to do: turn someone comparing three or four local tradespeople into someone who messages you.

The trouble is that a lot of decorators' websites were built years ago, or thrown together cheaply, and they don't do that job. They load slowly on a phone, they don't say clearly what areas are covered, and there's no obvious way to get in touch beyond a phone number buried in the footer. This article covers what the problem actually looks like, how a working website is put together, and how to judge whether yours is pulling its weight.

## The problem: why decorators lose work to their own website

A homeowner looking for an exterior painter after a damp winter, or a landlord needing a flat repainted between tenants, usually looks at a handful of options before picking up the phone. They're on their mobile, often in the evening, often in a hurry. If your site takes more than a couple of seconds to load, shows a grid of stock photos instead of real jobs, or doesn't say whether you cover their postcode, they move on to the next name.

Commercial clients behave differently again. A facilities manager booking repaint work for an office or a block of flats wants to see that you've done commercial jobs before, that you carry the right insurance, and that there's a straightforward way to request a quote without ringing round during office hours.

In both cases, the website is doing the selling before you ever speak to the customer. If it's slow, vague or out of date, you're relying entirely on referrals to make up the gap, and referrals alone rarely fill a diary during a quiet month.

## How a website for painters and decorators should work, step by step

A website that actually brings in enquiries follows a simple structure, built around what a homeowner or facilities manager needs to see before they'll get in touch.

1. **A clear homepage that states what you do and where.** Interior decorating, exterior painting, wallpapering, commercial work, whatever your mix is, it should be obvious within a few seconds, along with the towns and postcodes you cover.
2. **Separate pages for your main services.** A page on exterior painting can talk about weatherproof coatings, prep work and timing around the seasons. A wallpapering page can show pattern matching and feature walls. These pages give search engines something specific to match against what people are typing in.
3. **Real photos of real jobs.** Before and after shots of a repainted living room, a freshly coated render, a commercial corridor repainted overnight to avoid disrupting a business. Stock photography does the opposite of what you want: it makes a genuine, skilled tradesperson look generic.
4. **Reviews, placed where people will actually read them.** Not just a link to a review site, but a few genuine quotes on the homepage and service pages, close to where someone is deciding whether to contact you.
5. **An obvious way to get in touch**, repeated more than once: a phone number in the header, a simple enquiry form, and ideally a WhatsApp link, since many people would rather send a message than make a call.
6. **Fast loading on a phone**, because most enquiries start on a mobile and a site that drags will lose people before they see any of the above.

This is the same structure that works for other trades, it's just applied with decorating-specific detail: coverage area, job types, seasonal considerations like exterior work drying times, and the balance between domestic and commercial jobs if you do both.

## Common mistakes decorators make with their website

A few mistakes show up again and again on decorators' sites, and each one costs enquiries.

- **No mention of area coverage.** If someone can't tell within seconds whether you cover their postcode, they'll assume you don't and look elsewhere.
- **Photos that don't match the work on offer.** A site advertising commercial repaints but only showing domestic lounge photos leaves a facilities manager unsure you've done the job before.
- **A contact form with too many fields.** Long forms asking for details better covered in a phone call put people off before they've started.
- **No reviews, or reviews hidden on a separate page.** Trust signals only work if they're seen at the moment someone is deciding whether to contact you.
- **A site that looks fine on a desktop but is slow or cramped on a phone.** Given that most searches happen on mobile, this is often the single biggest cause of lost enquiries.
- **Out of date information.** An old phone number, a defunct email address, or services you no longer offer sitting on the page undermines confidence straight away.

If any of these sound familiar, it's worth checking your current site on your own phone, pretending to be a customer, before you spend money on anything else.

## How to judge whether your website is working

The honest test is simple: is it producing enquiries, and are they the right kind? A website that gets visitors but no messages isn't doing its job, regardless of how it looks. A few practical checks:

- How many enquiries come through the contact form or WhatsApp each month, and how many turn into quotes?
- Are the enquiries from the areas and job types you actually want, or are you fielding calls from outside your coverage area?
- How long does someone wait for a reply after they get in touch? Slow responses lose jobs to whoever answers first.
- Does the site load quickly on a phone with an average connection, not just on office wifi?

If you're not tracking any of this at the moment, that's the starting point, not a new website. Once you know where the gaps are, whether it's slow loading, missing area pages, or enquiries going unanswered overnight, you can fix the actual problem rather than guessing. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a website for a painting and decorating business cost?

Costs vary depending on how many pages you need and whether you want extras like an AI WhatsApp assistant built in. The [web design pricing page](/web-design/pricing) sets out typical packages so you can see what's included at each level.

### Do I need separate pages for interior, exterior and commercial work?

If you do a reasonable amount of each, yes. Separate pages let you go into detail on each type of job, which helps both visitors and search engines understand exactly what you offer and where.

### Will a new website actually bring in more enquiries on its own?

A good website makes it easier for people who find you to contact you, but it doesn't put you in front of new customers by itself. Pairing it with local Facebook and Instagram advertising, or making sure it ranks for local searches, is usually what drives the volume.

### Should I include prices on my website?

Most decorators avoid listing fixed prices because jobs vary so much in size and prep work needed. A rough guide to typical job ranges can help set expectations, but a quote request form is usually the better route to an accurate price.

### How quickly should I reply to enquiries from my website?

As quickly as possible. People comparing several decorators often go with whoever replies first, which is why many businesses now use an automated first response, like an AI WhatsApp assistant, to acknowledge and qualify enquiries the moment they arrive, even outside working hours.
