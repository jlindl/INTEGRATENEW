Most heating engineers get work from three places: repeat customers, referrals, and whatever Google throws up when a boiler packs in. That works, but it is unpredictable. A cold snap brings a flood of breakdown calls, then the phone goes quiet for weeks. Facebook ads for heating engineers exist to smooth that out, by putting your business in front of homeowners in your service area before they go searching for a stranger online.

Done properly, this is not about boosting a post and hoping. It is a targeted system: an ad shown to people in your area, a form that captures the details you need, and a way to respond before someone else does. This article walks through how it actually works, what good looks like, and where heating engineers commonly go wrong with it.

## Why word of mouth and Google alone leave gaps

Referrals are the best leads a heating engineer gets. The trouble is they do not arrive on a schedule. You cannot plan a quiet March around who happens to recommend you that month.

Google has the opposite problem. Someone searching 'boiler repair near me' already has a broken boiler and wants someone now. That is valuable, but it is also reactive and competitive, especially if you are bidding against national boiler companies with bigger budgets.

Facebook and Instagram ads work differently. They reach homeowners based on where they live, not what they happen to type into a search bar at that exact moment. That means you can get in front of someone whose boiler is ten years old and due for servicing, or a household that has been putting off quotes for a heat pump, before they start actively comparing engineers. It also means you can keep a steady flow of enquiries going through the quieter months, rather than relying entirely on breakdowns.

## How facebook ads for heating engineers actually work

The mechanics are straightforward once you break them down into steps.

1. **The ad is shown to people in your service area.** You set a radius or a list of postcodes, so the ad only reaches homeowners within a realistic travel distance of your business, not people three counties away who will never book you.
2. **The ad speaks to a specific job.** A generic 'heating engineer available' ad gets ignored. An ad about annual boiler servicing before winter, or one aimed at households still on an old gas boiler who might be curious about heat pump grants, gets noticed because it is relevant to the person seeing it.
3. **The click leads to an instant form, not a website.** Instead of sending someone to a homepage where they have to find a contact page and type an email, the ad opens a short form right there on Facebook or Instagram: name, phone number, postcode, and a couple of questions about the job.
4. **The enquiry lands with the details you need to quote.** A form that asks 'is this a repair, a service, or a new installation' and 'roughly when do you need this done' saves you the back and forth of a vague message that just says 'need a boiler'.
5. **Someone, or something, replies straight away.** The gap between enquiry and reply matters enormously in this trade. If a boiler has stopped working, the homeowner is likely messaging two or three engineers at once. Whoever replies first and sounds competent usually gets the job.

That last step is where a lot of heating engineers lose leads they have already paid for. You can run a well-targeted ad, get a strong response, and still watch the enquiry go cold because nobody saw the message until the next morning. This is why the form is often connected to an automated WhatsApp reply that confirms receipt, asks the qualifying questions, and gets a job onto your calendar, rather than leaving a lead sitting in your inbox. ## What a good campaign looks like for heating engineers

A well-run campaign for a heating engineer usually has a few things in common.

**The targeting matches reality.** If you cover a 15 to 20 mile radius and rarely travel further for a boiler install, the ad should not be reaching people 40 miles away. Wasted reach is wasted budget.

**The offer matches the season.** Servicing and boiler health checks make sense to advertise in late summer and early autumn, before the cold weather hits and everyone's attention turns to breakdowns. Heat pump enquiries tend to come from homeowners doing research over a longer period, so those ads can run more steadily through the year. A single generic ad running all year round rarely performs as well as a few that are tied to what people are actually thinking about at that time.

**The form asks the right questions, and no more.** Property type, whether it is a repair or install, roughly when they want it done, and a contact number is usually enough. Asking ten questions before someone can submit an enquiry just loses them halfway through.

**The reply is fast.** Whether that is you personally, someone in the office, or an automated system, the gap between the form being submitted and the first reply should be measured in minutes, not hours. ## Common mistakes heating engineers make with Facebook ads

A few patterns come up again and again when heating engineers try this without a clear structure behind it.

- **Sending the click to a homepage instead of a form.** Every extra step between seeing the ad and submitting an enquiry loses a proportion of interested people. A homepage asks someone to do more work than they are willing to do on their phone, mid-scroll.
- **Targeting too broadly.** Setting the radius to an entire city or region, rather than the actual area the business realistically serves, means paying to reach people who were never going to book.
- **Running one ad and leaving it.** Ad performance drops over time as the same audience sees it repeatedly. Campaigns need refreshing with new images, new angles, and new seasonal offers every few weeks.
- **No plan for what happens after the enquiry.** This is the big one. A form submission is not a booked job. If nobody follows up within the hour, especially on an urgent breakdown, the homeowner has often already booked someone else.
- **Treating every enquiry the same.** A boiler breakdown needs a same-day or next-day response. A heat pump enquiry is usually a longer decision, and chasing it too hard too fast can put people off. The follow-up approach should match the type of job.

## How to judge whether it is working

The honest answer is to look past vanity numbers like clicks and reach, and focus on what actually matters to the business.

- **Cost per qualified enquiry**, not cost per form fill. A cheap lead that turns out to be outside your area or not a real job is not actually cheap.
- **Enquiry to booked job rate.** If plenty of enquiries come in but few turn into booked work, the issue is usually in the follow-up, not the ads themselves.
- **Job mix.** Are you getting a sensible balance of servicing, repairs and installs, or is the campaign only pulling in one type of job when you need a spread?
- **Response time.** Track how long it takes, on average, from enquiry to first reply. This is one of the clearest levers you have for turning more enquiries into booked jobs.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a heating engineer spend on Facebook ads?

There is no fixed figure that suits every business, because it depends on your area, how many jobs you can realistically take on, and what a booked job is worth to you. The more useful question is cost per qualified enquiry, and whether that figure leaves you with a healthy margin once a job is won.

### Will Facebook ads work for heat pump installs, which take longer to decide on?

Yes, but the approach differs from a boiler breakdown ad. Heat pump enquiries usually need content that explains the process and addresses common questions, and a slower, more informative follow-up, rather than the fast, urgent response style that works for breakdowns.

### Do I need a website for this to work?

Not strictly, since the ad can send people straight to a lead form without touching a website at all. That said, a decent website still helps once someone wants to check you out before booking, particularly for bigger jobs like heat pump installations or full central heating upgrades.

### What happens if I cannot answer every enquiry straight away?

This is the most common reason ad campaigns underperform. An automated WhatsApp reply that confirms the enquiry and asks the key qualifying questions keeps the conversation moving even when you are up a ladder or mid-job, so the lead does not go cold before you get a chance to call back.

### How long before I can tell if a campaign is working?

Give it a few weeks rather than a few days. Early results can be noisy, and it takes some running time to see a reliable pattern in cost per enquiry, job mix, and booking rate before you can judge it fairly.
